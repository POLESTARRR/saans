Every app shows you the city's air quality. None of them know that you burn a coil beside your bed, fry at eight, sweep with a jhaadu, and open the windows at exactly the wrong hour. This works out what you are actually breathing, and which single change would fix the most of it.

If you are buying one

The filters usually cost more than the machine

Sized from your actual room volume rather than the coverage claim on the box, then ranked on what it costs to own for five years. Replacement filters run ₹1,500 to ₹4,000 and clog faster in Indian air than the rated life suggests, which is why the cheap machine is often the expensive one.

Being honest about this

What this model cannot know

The emission figures are from literature, not from your kitchen. Every one is listed in data/sources.json with its source and a confidence rating. Frying and sweeping vary enormously between homes, and those two are marked low confidence for that reason.

Outdoor readings come from the nearest government monitor, which may be several kilometres away. Near a construction site or an arterial road your real exposure is higher than any station says.

The daily shape is a typical winter inversion curve, not a live hourly feed. It is right about the pattern, dawn peak and afternoon trough, and approximate about any particular hour.

PM2.5 only. NO2 from gas burners, ozone and VOCs are real and not modelled here.
